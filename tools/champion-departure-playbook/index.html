Rebuild
Four criteria, weighted.

A second relationship carries the most weight. A rebuild with no one to rebuild to is not a rebuild.

A second relationship in the account WEIGHT ×3

Someone else inside the account knows you, trusts the product, and will take your call. Not just the champion.

The successor is known WEIGHT ×2

You know who decides now, or who will. A name, not a title.

Value is documented WEIGHT ×2

The value is written down in a form the successor can see. Results, not slides.

Renewal date and decision process known WEIGHT ×2

You know when the renewal lands and who signs it. The date and the decider.

        How I think about this: a single-threaded account has no champion, it has a point of failure. My rule of thumb: 0–20% dependency, we can survive a departure; 21–50%, some risk; 51–80%, high risk, widen relationships now; 81–100%, crisis mode. This playbook is what you run when the crisis arrives.

        Say it once, clearly: noticing is worthless unless you say it out loud. When the champion leaves, someone has to say it in the room, once, clearly, before the account goes quiet. That sentence is the first step of the rebuild.
