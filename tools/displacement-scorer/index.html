The displacement
Six criteria, weighted.

Workflow dependency carries the most weight. If the buyer's team doesn't depend on you daily, you have no wedge — you have a feature comparison, and the incumbent wins those.

Workflow dependency WEIGHT ×3

The buyer's team depends on our workflows daily, not just our reports.

Integration depth WEIGHT ×2

We're integrated into their stack: data flowing, not a sidecar.

Incumbent gaps WEIGHT ×2

The incumbent has real gaps the buyer feels: missing capability, cost, or friction.

Switching cost WEIGHT ×2

Switching cost is quantified and the buyer accepts it — migration path, not a leap.

Champion + economic buyer WEIGHT ×2

A champion and the economic buyer both want the change.

Proof against their data WEIGHT ×2

We've proven it against the buyer's own data, not a canned demo.

    How I use this: the play isn't to kill the incumbent. It's to become load-bearing next to it. The incumbent finds the outage; you show what it cost the business. Once the buyer can't do their job without you, the displacement conversation starts itself — and "cut the redundant vendor" becomes "cut the tool your engineers click into from every alert," which is a much harder sell internally. That is the wedge.
