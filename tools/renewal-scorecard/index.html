Score the renewal on the technical case, not the check-ins. If the product isn't working, the value isn't visible, or one person holds the whole relationship, no amount of relationship saves it.

Section A — Champion dependency
One person, or a relationship?

Answer Yes where a single person owns it. The stronger your champion feels, the more dangerous it is — if they leave, the value leaves with them.

Introduced us

A single person introduced us to the company.

Runs training

A single person runs internal training.

Handles invoices & renewals

A single person handles invoices and renewal questions.

First call on issues

A single person is who we contact when there's an issue.

Attends the QBRs

A single person attends the quarterly business reviews.

Champion dependency questions after Kelly M.'s Champion Dependency Score: 0–20% we can survive a departure, 21–50% some risk, 51–80% high risk, 81–100% crisis mode.

Section B — Value realized
Can the customer say what it did for them?

Value isn't a feeling, it's a chain: goals decoded, roadmap, quick wins, measured, demonstrated, monetized. Score each step honestly.

Goals decoded

Customer goals are identified, and how they're measured.

Roadmap

There's a roadmap of the steps the customer needs to take, and the inputs we provide.

Quick wins

Onboarding delivered quick wins that bought buy-in.

Measured

Progress is tracked on metrics the customer cares about, with deviations caught early.

Demonstrated

Actual results are benchmarked against projections and shown to the customer.

Growth identified

Growth opportunities are identified, with scenarios for the gain.

Value steps after Markus Rentsch's Customer Value-Led Growth: decode, operationalize, enable, measure, demonstrate, monetize.

Section C — Technical health
Is the product actually working for them?

This is the renewal case. Activity metrics and check-in counts don't answer it — the product either works for them or it doesn't.

It works

No unresolved defects or performance problems blocking users.

Adoption is deep

The right users on the right features — not one power user and shelfware.

Support load is normal

The account isn't eating a disproportionate share of support time.

Usage is stable or growing

Usage trend is flat-to-up, not declining.
